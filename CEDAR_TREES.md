# Cedar Trees

Cedars are large, long-lived coniferous (cone-bearing) evergreen trees prized for
their fragrant, durable wood and striking, tiered silhouette. The name "cedar"
is applied both to the "true cedars" of the genus *Cedrus* and, more loosely, to
several unrelated aromatic conifers in other families.

## True Cedars (*Cedrus*)

The genus *Cedrus* (family Pinaceae) contains four species native to the
mountains of the Mediterranean region and the western Himalayas:

| Species | Common Name | Native Range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Cedrus libani* | Lebanon Cedar | Lebanon, Syria, Turkey |
| *Cedrus atlantica* | Atlas Cedar | Atlas Mountains of Morocco & Algeria |
| *Cedrus deodara* | Deodar Cedar | Western Himalayas |
| *Cedrus brevifolia* | Cyprus Cedar | Cyprus |

## Identifying Features

- **Needles:** Short, stiff needles borne in dense spiral clusters (rosettes) on
  short shoots, with single needles along young long shoots.
- **Cones:** Distinctive barrel-shaped, upright cones that sit on the branches
  like eggs and disintegrate on the tree at maturity, releasing winged seeds.
- **Bark:** Grey-brown, developing deep fissures and scaly plates with age.
- **Form:** Broad, spreading, often flat-topped or tiered crowns in mature trees,
  with a strong horizontal branching habit.

## Habitat and Growth

Cedars thrive in well-drained soils on sunny mountain slopes and can tolerate
cold winters and dry summers. They are slow-growing but exceptionally
long-lived, with some specimens surviving well over 1,000 years and reaching
heights of 40 meters (130 feet) or more.

## Uses

- **Timber:** The aromatic, rot- and insect-resistant heartwood has been valued
  since antiquity for building, shipbuilding, and fine furniture.
- **Aromatics:** Cedarwood oil is used in perfumes, soaps, and as a natural
  insect repellent.
- **Ornamental:** Atlas and Deodar cedars are widely planted as specimen trees
  in parks and large gardens for their graceful form.

## Cultural Significance

The Lebanon cedar (*Cedrus libani*) is one of the most storied trees in history.
It appears in the *Epic of Gilgamesh* and repeatedly in the Hebrew Bible, was
used to build temples and ships in the ancient Near East, and today features on
the national flag of Lebanon as a symbol of endurance and peace.

## "False" Cedars

Many trees carry the name "cedar" without belonging to *Cedrus*, including:

- **Western Red Cedar** (*Thuja plicata*) — family Cupressaceae
- **Eastern Red Cedar** (*Juniperus virginiana*) — a juniper
- **Spanish Cedar** (*Cedrela odorata*) — a flowering tree in the mahogany family

These share the aromatic, durable wood that earned the "cedar" name but are
botanically distinct from the true cedars.
